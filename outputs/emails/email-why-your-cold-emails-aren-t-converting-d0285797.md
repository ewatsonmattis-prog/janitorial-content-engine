Hi [First Name],

Most commercial cleaning companies that come to us have tried cold email before. They built a list, wrote a few emails, hit send — and heard nothing back.

So they concluded cold email doesn't work.

It does work. But not the way most cleaning businesses are using it.

Here's the problem: a single cold email to a facilities manager or office manager is almost never going to win you a contract. Not because your offer is weak. Because trust hasn't been built yet, and timing is rarely perfect on day one.

The companies consistently winning recurring office contracts from cold outreach aren't doing anything clever. They're doing something most of their competitors aren't: they're following up. Repeatedly. With value. With patience.

A prospect who ignores your first email isn't a dead lead. They're a future client who hasn't seen enough from you yet. A well-structured follow-up sequence — four to six touchpoints over three to four weeks — changes the entire dynamic. It signals reliability. It keeps you visible. And it gives the prospect a reason to respond when the timing finally works for them.

The other mistake is sending generic outreach. "We provide professional cleaning services at competitive rates" lands in the bin. What works is specificity — referencing the type of facility, the contract pain points that sector actually faces, and a clear reason why now is the right time to talk.

This is the difference between cold email as a one-off tactic and cold email as a system. One produces silence. The other builds a pipeline of conversations with decision-makers who already know who you are before they pick up the phone.
