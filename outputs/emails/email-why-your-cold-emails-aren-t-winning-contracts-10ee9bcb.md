**Preview Text:** It's not the list. It's what happens after the first email.

Hi [First Name],

Most cleaning companies sending cold emails are doing the same thing: one email, no reply, move on.

That's not a lead generation system. That's a lottery.

Here's what's actually happening. Facilities managers and office managers receive dozens of supplier emails every week. They don't ignore you because they're not interested. They ignore you because they're busy, and one email from a company they've never heard of doesn't give them enough reason to stop and respond.

The problem isn't your list. The problem is what happens — or doesn't happen — after that first email.

The cleaning companies winning recurring commercial contracts aren't necessarily sending better first emails. They're running a consistent follow-up sequence that builds familiarity over time. Three, four, five touchpoints. Each one short, relevant, and adding something — a specific result, a relevant case study, a direct question that's easy to answer.

This is what turns a cold prospect into a conversation.

There's also a trust gap that most cleaning companies don't account for. A facilities manager committing to a monthly contract is taking a risk. They need to believe you'll show up, communicate well, and fix problems when they arise. Your cold outreach needs to signal that — not just list your services and your price.

That means your emails should reference the type of buildings you work in, the contract sizes you handle, and ideally a specific outcome you've delivered for a similar client. Not a pitch. A proof point.

When you combine a targeted prospect list, a structured follow-up sequence, and messaging that builds credibility at each step, cold email stops feeling like a gamble and starts working like a pipeline.
