Hi [First Name],

Most commercial cleaning companies don't have a leads problem. They have a follow-up problem.

You've probably sent cold emails before. Maybe you got a few replies, maybe you didn't. Either way, the pipeline stayed empty and you moved on to the next thing. That's not a marketing failure — that's what happens when there's no system behind the outreach.

Here's the reality of selling commercial cleaning contracts in 2024: facilities managers and office managers are busy, sceptical, and inbox-overloaded. A single cold email — even a good one — almost never converts on its own. Research consistently shows it takes between five and eight touchpoints before a prospect engages seriously. Most cleaning companies give up after one or two.

The companies winning recurring office cleaning contracts aren't necessarily the ones with the best service. They're the ones who show up consistently, follow up without being annoying, and make it easy for the right prospect to say yes when the timing is right.

What does a proper cold outreach system look like? It starts with a targeted list — specific industries, specific locations, specific contract sizes you actually want. It pairs that with a short, direct email sequence that leads with a relevant pain point, not a features list. And it includes a follow-up cadence that keeps you visible without burning the relationship.

The goal isn't to flood inboxes. The goal is to build enough familiarity with the right decision-makers that when their current cleaning contractor lets them down — and eventually, they will — your name is the one they remember.

If your cold outreach isn't producing booked sales conversations, the problem usually isn't your service or your pricing. It's that there's no system keeping you in front of the right people long enough to earn their attention.
