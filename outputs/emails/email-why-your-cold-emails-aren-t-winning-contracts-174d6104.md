Hi [First Name],

Most commercial cleaning companies don't have a lead generation problem. They have a system problem.

You've probably sent cold emails before. Maybe you've tried a VA, a template from Google, or a spray-and-pray approach to local office parks. You got a few replies, followed up once or twice, then the thread went cold. Sound familiar?

The issue isn't that prospects aren't interested. It's that interest dies without a structured system to catch it.

Facilities managers and office managers are busy. They're not going to respond to your first email and immediately hand over a contract. They need to see your name more than once. They need to feel like you understand their problem. And they need a reason to say yes before they're even ready to switch providers.

A cold email by itself is just noise. A cold email backed by a follow-up sequence, a clear value proposition, and a consistent touchpoint strategy — that's a pipeline.

Here's what a working system actually looks like:

First, you're targeting the right decision-makers in the right sectors — offices, healthcare sites, schools, logistics hubs — not just anyone with a postcode.

Second, your emails lead with their problem, not your company history. Nobody cares how long you've been trading until they trust you.

Third, you follow up at least four to five times across different angles — not just "just checking in" — before writing off a prospect.

Fourth, when someone does show interest, there's a clear next step ready. Not a PDF. A booked call.

Most cleaning companies skip steps two, three, and four entirely. Then they conclude that cold email doesn't work. It works — when it's built properly.

If your outbound efforts have gone quiet or you've never had a consistent pipeline of commercial prospects, the fix isn't more leads. It's building the system that converts the ones you already have access to.
