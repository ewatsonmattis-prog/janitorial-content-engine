Hi [First Name],

Most cleaning company owners we speak to aren't short of leads. They're short of conversions.

They've tried email lists, LinkedIn messages, maybe even a lead generation company. A handful of prospects replied. Then nothing. The pipeline dried up, and the whole thing got written off as "not working."

Here's the problem: it wasn't the lead source. It was the system behind it.

Cold outreach for commercial cleaning contracts isn't a one-touch game. Facilities managers and office managers are busy. They're not sitting around waiting for your email. Most won't reply to the first message — not because they're not interested, but because the timing wasn't right, or they simply forgot.

The cleaning companies consistently winning recurring office contracts are the ones running structured follow-up sequences. Not pestering. Not blasting the same email five times. A considered sequence — a different angle, a relevant insight, a short case study — that keeps them warm until the moment they're ready to talk.

Trust is also built before the reply. The prospect who books a call with you has usually read three of your emails, seen your name twice in their inbox, and decided you sound credible. Generic outreach doesn't build that. A system built around your positioning, your target sectors, and your strongest proof points does.

The other mistake is treating every prospect the same. An office manager at a 50-person accountancy firm has different concerns than a facilities manager overseeing six commercial sites. Your messaging should reflect that. Segmented outreach — even simple segmentation — consistently outperforms broadcast emails.

If your cold outreach is generating interest but not booked conversations, the fix isn't more volume. It's building a follow-up sequence that converts interest into a calendar slot.
