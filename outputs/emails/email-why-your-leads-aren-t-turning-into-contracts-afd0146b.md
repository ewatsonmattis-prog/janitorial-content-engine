Hi [First Name],

Most cleaning companies aren't short on leads. They're short on a system that does anything useful with them.

You get an enquiry. You send a quote. You hear nothing back. You follow up once, maybe twice, then move on. That contract goes to a competitor who stayed in front of the decision-maker long enough to be the obvious choice when the time came.

This is the gap that costs cleaning companies thousands in recurring revenue every year — and it has nothing to do with your pricing or your service quality.

The problem is the pipeline, not the product.

When we work with commercial cleaning companies, one of the first things we look at is what happens after initial contact. Not the cleaning spec, not the quote template — the follow-up sequence. In most cases, there isn't one. There's a CRM with cold contacts, a few sent emails, and no structured reason for a prospect to respond.

A cold email campaign that generates interest is only the start. The companies winning recurring office cleaning contracts aren't necessarily the best at cleaning. They're the best at staying present, building credibility over time, and making it easy for a facilities manager or office director to say yes when the moment arrives.

What that looks like in practice: a short, direct cold email sequence targeting the right decision-makers. A follow-up cadence spaced across several weeks. A reason to reply at each touchpoint — not just a chase. And a conversation-first approach that earns trust before you ever talk price.

This isn't complicated. But it does need to be intentional, consistent, and built around how commercial buyers actually make decisions — which is slowly, cautiously, and only when they trust the company in front of them.

If your pipeline is full of contacts that haven't converted in months, the answer isn't more leads. It's a system that works the ones you already have.
