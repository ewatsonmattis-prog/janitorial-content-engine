Hi [First Name],

Most cleaning company owners tell me the same thing: they need more leads.

But when I dig into what's actually happening, the problem isn't lead volume. It's what happens after the first contact.

Someone opens your email. They're mildly interested. They don't reply. And you never follow up — because you've already moved on to the next batch of cold prospects.

That's not a lead generation problem. That's a system problem.

Here's what actually works for winning office cleaning contracts through cold email:

First, your initial email should do one thing — start a conversation. Not pitch your company history. Not list your accreditations. Just identify a relevant pain point for that prospect and ask a single, low-friction question. Short. Specific. Easy to reply to.

Second, most contracts are won in the follow-up. Not the first email. Research consistently shows that 80% of sales require five or more touchpoints before a decision is made. Most cleaning businesses send one email and stop. That's leaving contracts on the table.

Third, your follow-up sequence needs to add value, not just nudge. Each touchpoint should give the prospect a reason to engage — a relevant insight, a result you've helped a similar business achieve, or a simple reason why now is a good time to talk. Not "just checking in."

Fourth, once someone does show interest — an email open, a link click, a reply — that's a signal. That prospect needs to move into a faster, more personalised follow-up track. Speed and relevance at that stage can be the difference between booking a meeting and losing them to a competitor.

Finally, none of this works without consistency. A cold email system that runs for three weeks and stops delivers nothing. The cleaning companies winning commercial contracts through outreach are the ones running structured, repeatable sequences — month after month.

You don't need more generic leads. You need a system that builds trust, follows up properly, and turns interest into booked sales conversations.
