# Email Newsletter: Why your cold emails aren't converting

**Subject Line:** Why your cold emails aren't converting
**Preview Text:** It's not the list. It's what happens after the first send.
**Word Count:** 342

---

Hi [First Name],

Most commercial cleaning companies that struggle with lead generation aren't short on effort. They're short on system.

You've probably sent cold emails before. Maybe you bought a list, wrote something reasonable, hit send — and heard nothing back. So you moved on. That's the problem.

One email is not a campaign. One email is a guess.

The facilities managers and office decision-makers you're trying to reach are busy. They're not ignoring you because they don't need cleaning services. They're ignoring you because they don't know you yet, and you disappeared before they had a reason to respond.

What actually works is a sequence — a structured series of touchpoints that builds familiarity before it asks for anything. Subject line, opener, value, follow-up, follow-up again. Each message earns a little more attention than the last. By the time you ask for a conversation, you're not a stranger.

But sequencing alone isn't enough. The message has to be relevant. "We offer competitive rates and a reliable service" lands in the bin. "We work with property managers across [city] who've switched from their previous contractor after ongoing complaints — here's what changed" lands in the inbox of someone who's been having that exact problem.

Specificity is what separates cleaning companies that win contracts from cleaning companies that send emails.

On top of that, your follow-up needs to be consistent without being pushy. Most decision-makers need five to eight touchpoints before they're ready to talk. Most cleaning companies give up after two. That gap is where contracts are lost — and where a proper outbound system pays for itself.

This isn't about sending more emails. It's about building a repeatable process that moves prospects from cold to conversation without you having to chase every reply manually.

If your current approach to cold outreach feels like shouting into a void, it's worth looking at the system — not just the message.
