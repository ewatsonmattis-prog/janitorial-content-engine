**Subject Line:** Why your cold emails aren't winning contracts
**Preview Text:** It's not your list. It's what happens after the first message.
**Word Count:** 323

Hi [First Name],

Most commercial cleaning companies sending cold emails are making the same mistake.

They send one message, get no reply, and move on. Then they wonder why their pipeline is empty and they're still chasing the same few referrals they've had for years.

The problem isn't cold email. The problem is treating it like a one-shot lottery ticket.

Here's what actually works.

Facilities managers, office managers, and property directors are busy people. They're not sitting at their desk waiting for a cleaning quote. When your email lands, they might be in a meeting, dealing with a staffing issue, or already halfway through a contract renewal conversation with their current supplier.

That doesn't mean they're not interested. It means your timing was off.

This is why follow-up is where contracts are won. Research consistently shows that most B2B responses come after the third, fourth, or fifth touchpoint — not the first. If you're not following up at least three times across different angles, you're leaving contracts on the table.

But follow-up only works if the sequence is built properly. Each message needs to add something — a relevant observation, a question, a short proof point. Not just 'just checking in'. That gets deleted immediately.

The cleaning companies that consistently win office contracts aren't necessarily the best at cleaning. They're the best at staying visible, building familiarity, and showing up at the right moment with the right message.

That requires a system. Not a spreadsheet. Not a one-off email blast. A repeatable outbound process that identifies the right prospects, reaches out with a relevant message, follows up with intent, and routes warm responses into a booked sales conversation.

If your current approach to lead generation is inconsistent — some months you're prospecting, some months you're not — that's the core issue to fix first.

Predictable contract growth starts with a predictable outbound system. Everything else follows from that.
