Hi [First Name],

Most commercial cleaning companies don't have a lead generation problem. They have a follow-up problem.

You've probably sent emails, made calls, maybe even had a few decent conversations with facilities managers or office managers. But then nothing. The prospect goes quiet, you move on, and that potential contract disappears into the pipeline graveyard.

Here's what's actually happening.

Facilities managers are busy. They're not ignoring you because they're not interested — they're ignoring you because you reached out once, didn't hear back, and stopped. Meanwhile, a competitor who followed up four times over three weeks just booked the site visit.

Generic lead lists and one-shot cold emails don't win recurring contracts. What wins contracts is a system — one that reaches the right decision-makers, sends personalised outreach, and follows up consistently until you either get a yes, a no, or a clear signal to move on.

A working cold email system for commercial cleaning should do three things.

First, it should target by vertical. Offices, schools, healthcare facilities, and warehouses all have different pain points. Your outreach should speak directly to each one — not send the same email to all of them.

Second, it should follow up automatically, at least four to five times per sequence. Most positive replies in cold outreach come after the third or fourth touchpoint. If you're stopping at one, you're leaving contracts on the table.

Third, it should build trust incrementally. Each email in your sequence should add a small amount of value — a relevant insight, a social proof point, a question that shows you understand their situation. Not just "following up to see if you had a chance to read my last email."

The cleaning companies winning the most recurring commercial contracts right now aren't necessarily the best at cleaning. They're the best at showing up consistently in front of the right people, at the right time, with the right message.

If your current outreach isn't doing that, it's time to fix the system — not just send more emails.
