Hi [First Name],

Most cleaning companies chasing new office contracts have the same problem — and it's not a shortage of leads.

They get enquiries. They send quotes. Then nothing. The prospect goes quiet, the owner moves on, and the pipeline stays empty.

Here's what's actually happening.

A facilities manager or office contact sees your name — maybe from a cold email, a LinkedIn message, or a Google search. They're mildly interested. But they're not ready to switch contractors this week. So they file it away, forget it, and six months later they sign with someone else.

Not because that company was better. Because that company followed up.

The businesses winning recurring commercial contracts aren't sending better cold emails. They're running a system that keeps them visible between the first touch and the signed contract. That means structured follow-up sequences, not one-off chases. It means sending something useful — a case study, a relevant result, a short observation about their building type — not just "just checking in".

It means treating a cold prospect like a warm relationship that hasn't started yet.

Most cleaning company owners don't have this system. They're relying on memory, a spreadsheet, or a CRM they set up once and never maintained. So the leads that could become contracts quietly disappear.

A proper cold email lead generation system does three things: it gets the right people into your pipeline in the first place, it follows up with enough consistency that you stay front of mind, and it creates enough trust that when the timing is right, you're the obvious call.

That's not complicated. But it does need to be built deliberately.

If your pipeline feels unpredictable — busy one month, dry the next — the issue probably isn't lead volume. It's the system handling those leads after the first contact.
