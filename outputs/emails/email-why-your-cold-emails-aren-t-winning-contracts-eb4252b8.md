Hi [First Name],

Most commercial cleaning companies we speak to have tried cold email at some point. A list of office managers, a few hundred sends, maybe a handful of replies — then silence. So they conclude cold email doesn't work.

It's not the channel that's broken. It's the system behind it.

Here's what's actually happening: facilities managers and office decision-makers receive dozens of supplier emails every week. A single cold email — no matter how well written — rarely converts on its own. The companies winning contracts through cold outreach aren't better writers. They're more consistent.

The difference is a follow-up sequence that builds familiarity without being annoying. Three to five touchpoints, spaced across two to three weeks, each one adding a small reason to respond. Not chasing. Not pushing. Just staying visible until the timing is right.

Timing matters more than most people realise. The majority of commercial cleaning contracts get reviewed at renewal — and that window is short. If you're not in front of the right decision-maker before they start looking, someone else will be. A consistent outreach system keeps you present across a much larger pool of prospects than manual follow-up ever could.

The other piece most cleaning businesses skip is the trust signal. Before a facilities manager will hand over a recurring contract, they need to believe you're credible and low-risk. That means your emails should reference the kinds of clients you already work with, the contract sizes you handle, and the specific problems you solve — not just that you offer competitive rates and a professional service.

Generic positioning kills response rates. Specific, relevant positioning starts conversations.

If your outreach is generating some replies but not converting them into booked calls, the gap is usually in the follow-up and the credibility signals — not the initial email.
